What this tool will and won't tell you

Every food here comes from our own lunchboxes. The facts about each one are general and widely documented — what it contains, how it behaves when cooked or packed.

It gives no health advice and no scores, and it does not recommend meals. Questions marked planned have not been measured.

Mediterranean and Caspian combinations

Dishes from Azerbaijani, Caspian and Mediterranean kitchens that already put foods together, and the question each pairing raises for the lab. Questions, not results — none of these has been measured by us.

Noxud + limon
Published evidence · not our measurement

Hummus: chickpeas + lemon

Chickpeas carry plant (non-haem) iron; lemon juice carries vitamin C. Published single-meal studies show vitamin C can increase how much non-haem iron is absorbed. But chickpeas and tahini also contain phytate, which binds iron — so one dish holds both a helper and a blocker.

The same question as Pair 01: lentils + bell pepper.

Pomidor + zeytun yağı
Published evidence · not our measurement

Tomato + olive oil

Lycopene, the red pigment in tomato, dissolves in fat, not water. Published studies report that more lycopene is absorbed when tomato is eaten with oil, and from cooked tomato than from raw — the opposite of vitamin C, which heat destroys.

Links to our planned egg + tomato pairing, which compares raw and cooked tomato.

Tabbouleh
Question for the lab

Bulgur + parsley + lemon

Wheat and parsley both carry plant iron; parsley and lemon both carry vitamin C. The dish is eaten raw, so no vitamin C is lost to cooking. Does iron come out of it in the digestion model the way the published evidence would suggest?

A Mediterranean salad close to the herb salad in Box 03.

Cacıq
Planned measurement

Yogurt + cucumber + dill

Eaten as cacıq in Azerbaijan and as tzatziki around the Mediterranean. Yogurt is acidic; cucumber is about 95% water. Our planned pH measurement asks how far mixing them shifts the acidity.

Needs to stay cool until lunch. See dovğa, the warm yogurt-and-herb relative.

Borani
Published evidence · not our measurement

Spinach + yogurt

A Caspian dish of cooked spinach folded into yogurt. Spinach contains oxalate, which binds calcium into an insoluble salt — published studies show that calcium from spinach is poorly absorbed for this reason. Does the spinach's oxalate also bind calcium from the yogurt?

A combination where two foods may work against each other.

Balıq + narşərab
Planned measurement

Fish + something sour

On the Caspian coast, fish such as kütüm is served with narşərab, a sour pomegranate sauce; around the Mediterranean, grilled fish comes with lemon. Our planned pH measurement asks how much the sour partner changes the acidity of the mixture.

Cooked fish, like meat, must be kept cool until it is eaten.

Published evidence is described in general terms and is not a NUTRIX LAB result. Nothing here is dietary advice.

How the library is built

The library is a lookup over our own records. It is deliberately small: it only grows as our lunchboxes, archive and lab work do.

  1. Foods from our boxes

    A food enters the library when it appears in one of our photographed lunchboxes, or the team names it as a lunchbox food.

  2. Food groups

    Each food is placed in one group — starchy, protein, dairy, fruit and vegetables, or herbs and spices — the way public food guides group them.

  3. Combinations

    "Found together" means the foods were packed in the same photographed box. Nothing else counts as a documented combination.

  4. Questions, not results

    Questions link to our pairings, our planned measurements or the archive. When a result is measured, it will be marked as ours, with its date.
